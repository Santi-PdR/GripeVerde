# Survival diary

## 14 October 1800
**The year the streets started to smell wrong**

This begins in 1800, though the customs papers call it something else. A new infection appeared and the governments did what they always do: they covered it with a small word. "Common flu", they said. We all believed it once, as children, in the kitchen at home, with a hot forehead and a cup of broth.

I believed it too. I write the date because somebody has to write it: the day it began, no one else will remember.

The city's physicians talk about vapours, about corrupted air rising from the drains. They may be right or they may not: at the lazaretto they count forty days and nobody argues with the number. Forty days is how long a quarantine has lasted since Ragusa imposed one in 1377, and the word comes from there, from "quaranta giorni", forty days of waiting on the water before coming ashore.

## 22 October
**The Great Lie: what on earth this fever is**

The fallen here are not dead. I write it slowly, because it is the only thing worth understanding: the sickness does not raise corpses. It is a mutated virus, a rabies that learned to travel through the air, crossed with the flu of every winter.

The government's emergency agency found out. And to keep the streets from panic it gave it a small name: flu. Some say "green flu" for the colour the skin turns under a lamp, and the name stuck.

The infected feel no pain. Their blood fills with an adrenaline that is not human: they run like post horses and shove like oxen. We call them Ghouls. Bluish skin, blue eyes glowing in the dark as if a candle burned behind them.

~~ That they can be reasoned with, that talking is enough. A lie from the consulates.

## 3 November
**What they say in the ports**

When the news went around the world, every alarm went off. The countries that still lack the craft will fare worse than anyone. Napoleon Bonaparte, who since last year is First Consul and has not crowned himself yet, and the other kings are in fatal trouble: an ordered army is useless against something that does not respect ranks.

And the sickness does not sit still. It mutates with the habits of each country, with blood and with the health of each person. Every region breeds a different offspring.

- Margin note: they say there is an intelligence system that records everything and lets you plan a thousand ideas. If you see someone attempting an action in chat and you feel like testing whether you can do the same: don't. Everything has its requirements, and there are many.

## 9 November
**Where it came from: the brig's cargo**

On the old quay everyone tells the same story, each with their own version. A cargo brig came from Danzig -a port of wheat, tallow and amber, in the Baltic- with full holds and one rat too many. It was unloaded at night, without quarantine, because quarantine costs money and wheat waits.

Three weeks later, the quay district had a fever. In 1793, in Philadelphia, yellow fever came in the same way and killed thousands; back then they closed the port and burned tar on the corners. Here nothing was closed.

- The fever did not come from a laboratory or a castle: it came on a ship, with the cargo and the passage. That is how Hamburg's cholera arrived in 1892 and Porto's plague in 1899, and that is how this one arrived.
- There is no vaccine yet and there will not be one for a long time: rabies did not get its own until 1885, and I know that because an old physician who reads forbidden books told me so.

## 15 November
**Rules of the trade: falling is not dying**

When your hearts reach zero you do not die: you fall. You lie on the ground, incapacitated, looking at boots and cobblestones.

Another member of the team has to come close and hold the interaction for five whole seconds, without taking a single hit, to lift you up.

If the incapacitation bar reaches zero, that is it: real death, spectator mode, no inventory loss. The body stays where it fell, as a reminder for whoever comes next. To bring the dead back you need a defibrillator, and those only come out of loot.

---

## 22 November
**The PINs**

There are attacks from special mutations that cancel control entirely. They grab you and that is that: you can do nothing, and no bright idea will get you out. Every attempt is closed, even the clever ones.

Only your team can free you. That is why exploring alone is a guaranteed death sentence. And when someone is caught, a global warning sounds that everyone hears: that bell means a teammate is hanging from a pin.

---

## 29 November
**Where these things come from (I): the Ghoul**

The most basic mutation: the being the virus turns first. An ordinary walker, with blue skin and blue eyes.

The word was not invented by the agency. It comes from the Arabic "ghul", the one who lurks in the pass and changes shape, and it entered European books with the translations of "One Thousand and One Nights" that children were reading a hundred years ago. The name fits: it does not reason, it lurks.

What the blood gives it is endurance, strength, speed and less knockback when it strikes. One alone lands three hits in a second and a half. Its only passive ability is reach: three blocks of distance.

And they pile up. When one starts stacking blocks to escape, they climb on each other until they reach him.

- Difficulty: 1 skull.

---

## 6 December
**Where these things come from (II): the Jockey**

One of the first specials with a grab. It is small, it looks like a dreadful monkey, and it attacks by clinging from behind or in front: it waits, it distracts you with its body, and it takes you by surprise.

The name comes from the racing trade: a jockey is the light rider who mounts the animal's back and steers it where he wants. This one does the same with people. It shifts its weight to make you stumble wherever it wants, and it only lets go if it is killed.

It can drag you to natural hazards, to the witches that weep in the yards, to other infected, or straight to the edge of a cliff so you hang there or so it kills you if you do not fight back.

- Difficulty: 2 skulls.

---

## 13 December
**Where these things come from (III): the Cuirassier**

This one is not a rag. It is a short-range enemy, with massive health, armed with a heavy sabre and dressed in a metal cuirass.

The cuirass is no theatre prop: it is what French heavy cavalry wore in this time, an iron plate over the chest, a straight sabre and a big horse. The man died with it on and the mutation kept it: that is why bullets do not go in.

Its most dangerous ability is the charge: it roars, accelerates to an extreme speed and rams in a straight line or a curve, dealing massive damage by impalement. Whoever it runs through is left in a Stabbed state: on the ground and bleeding for a good while. No defibrillator or first aid kit fixes that.

It blocks every kind of projectile and returns them to whoever fired, with damage multiplied by 1.25. It can even block melee attacks if it guesses your movement: that is why closing in with a blade is now the only real way to hurt it.

- Field note: against one or two survivors it eats your time and keeps you busy. Against four or more it becomes a vulnerable lobster, but it will choose when to use the charge.
- Difficulty: 5 skulls.

## 20 December
**The cordon and the lime**

When the fever settled in, the city did what has been done since Ragusa: it closed the way and counted forty days. It set a cordon of guards on the exits, opened the lazaretto for suspect ships and handed out quicklime and vinegar on every corner, because corrupted air, they say, is killed by whatever burns.

It worked halfway. The cordon held where there were armed men and discipline, and broke where there was hunger. That is the whole history of quarantine, in any century.

- What does work, and I learned it the hard way: whoever comes back from the street washes before touching the door, the report is signed with what one saw and not with what one was told, and the house registry is never thrown away. Paper is the only thing that outlives the witnesses.

## 27 December
**Races: what we carry inside**

On first entering one can be born human, Shark, Cyborg, or one more of the 300+ races in the catalogue. They support the player: an extra help to fight high difficulties.

They evolve: V1, V2, V3, V4 and beyond. Each stage asks for actions or missions according to the race's rarity.

- Do you have a race? Check it with Review race rarity, between asterisks, at the start and at the end. A notification arrives with the rarity you drew.

Rarities, from the most common to the hardest to obtain, also catalogue artifacts and objects:

- Common, Uncommon, Rare, Ultra Rare, Legendary
- Obsainan, Mythic, Godly, Eternal, Fabled

## 31 December
**Difficulties, restarts and the light above your head**

Every difficulty change brings new challenges and global events. The most basic thing is that mobs raise their stats.

- Easy, Normal, Advanced, Expert
- INSURGENCY I-V, INFERNUM I-V
- BACKBREAKING I-X (in preparation)
- DOOMSDAY I-X (in preparation)
- ARMAGEDDON I-X (in preparation)

Every player carries a white icon above their head: it is there to find each other again. It holds for more than 3,000 blocks of distance and shows even if those chunks are not loaded yet.

The server keeps growing, with daily updates. That is why restarts are never missing: they are daily, but few.

## 1 January 1801
**Last page for now**

Left in some shelter, on an island, in a half-fallen building. Adapt your weapons, perfect your tactics and survive.

You will have enough resources to survive safe and sound. Or perhaps you will not have them, and in time they will run out.

I write so as not to forget. If anyone finds this notebook: it started in 1800, in the quay district, with the cargo of a brig nobody inspected. The lie was called flu. The ones who ran were not dead. And the city is still here, counting forty days.
